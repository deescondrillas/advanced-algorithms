The neighborhood library opens its doors at eight in the morning. Before the readers arrive, Helen checks the tables, arranges the chairs, and returns each book to its shelf. A sign near the entrance invites everyone to ask questions: knowing every title is not a requirement for finding a good story.

A student is looking for information about the oceans. A little girl wants a story about dragons, and a neighbor asks for a guide to repairing bicycles. Helen listens to each person and suggests different paths. Sometimes one book is enough; at other times, comparing several sources and writing questions in a notebook is more useful.

The reading club meets in the afternoon. Its members do not always agree about the ending of the novel, but they explain their reasons respectfully. When it is time to close, Helen looks at the notes left on the table. The library keeps books, and it also helps questions turn into conversations.
